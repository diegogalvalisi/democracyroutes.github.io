A FIELD GUIDE · DIGITAL DEMOCRACY

Democracy drives on conversation.
Software carries it further.

From assemblies, to the town halls, to the internet — the tools change, the work stays the same: deliberate together, decide together, count fairly. This guide maps the software built for exactly that job.

01

What the software actually does

Technology enhances democracy. It removes the excuses.

i.

It widens the room

participation

A town hall fits forty people on a Tuesday night. An online deliberation fits a whole city — shift workers, parents, the shy, the far away. Participation stops being a privilege of those with free time.

ii.

It makes disagreement legible

deliberation

Consensus-mapping tools turn a thousand messy opinions into a map: where the crowd agrees, where it splits, and what statements bridge the divide. The middle stops being invisible.

iii.

It counts without arguing

voting

Polls and votes with public, verifiable tallies. Everyone sees the same numbers, computed the same way. The arithmetic of democracy becomes boring — and boring is exactly what you want it to be.

iv.

It closes the loop

accountability

Most participation dies after the meeting: "we heard you" and nothing changes. Modern infrastructure can publish proposals, track decisions, and show citizens what their input actually did. Accountability as a feature, not a press release.

02

The software index

Real projects, running in real governments. Click to unfold.

▶

Decidim

participatory platform

Barcelona-born, open source, used by hundreds of cities.

The heavyweight of participatory democracy: participatory budgets, citizen initiatives, consultations, deliberation spaces, and result tracking in one modular platform. Barcelona processes thousands of citizen proposals through it — transparent down to the proposal level. Barcelona and 500+ organizations worldwide

▶

Polis

consensus mapping

An opinion survey that draws a map instead of a percentage.

Agree or disagree with short statements; machine learning clusters the crowd in real time and shows the majority and minority opinions — and the "bridge statements" both sides accept. No comments, no replies, no trolling: just positions. Taiwan's vTaiwan process

▶

Loomio

group decisions

A worker-run cooperative from New Zealand, for groups that must decide.

Discussion threads where anyone can raise a proposal, and the group votes: yes, no, abstain, block, or "needs discussion." The block matters — it forces the group to hear the objection instead of outvoting it. co-ops, boards, activist networks

▶

Consider.it

deliberation

Arguments for, arguments against, one slider between them.

Each participant lists the pros and cons that weigh on them, then places themselves on a spectrum. The visualization shows the distribution of positions and which arguments actually move people — the structure of disagreement, made visible. large-scale citizen deliberations

▶

Consul

civic participation

Madrid's open-source platform, now on three continents.

Debates, proposals, voting, participatory budgeting, and a signature system that lets citizens force official consideration of popular ideas. A city-hall experiment that became civic infrastructure. dozens of cities worldwide

▶

Ethelo

complex trade-offs

For questions where the options collide: budgets, boundaries.

Instead of a single winner-takes-all vote, Ethelo evaluates how options interact and finds the combination the group finds fairest — like solving a knapsack problem where the weights are people's values. community planning, climate consultations

04

The catch

Software for democracy fails like everything else: at the edges.

Tools don't create trust; they inherit it. A platform dropped into a community with no groundwork becomes an expensive suggestion box. Participation infrastructure needs organizers, offline outreach, and a government that visibly responds — otherwise citizens learn that speaking up changes nothing, which is worse than never asking.

Digital dividesaccess

The loudest online voices are not the most affected. Weighting and outreach are political decisions hiding inside technical ones.

Gaming the tallyintegrity

Any public voting system can be flooded. The good tools publish methodology and resist Sybil attacks; the cheap ones just count clicks.

Deliberation theaterhonesty

A consultation whose outcome is predetermined is worse than none. Closing the loop — showing what the input changed — is the difference between participation and decoration.

05

The four modules

One platform, four verbs. Everything else is configuration.

Assemblies & deliberation

core

Structured spaces where a group thinks before it decides. Proposals, objections, amendments and "needs discussion" states — the slow, visible work of changing minds.

proposal → discuss → amend
   ↘ objection? → resolve ↗
   └→ ready → escalate to vote

Polling & temperature

sensing

Lightweight, recurring measurement of where the crowd stands — not binding, always visible. Check the room's temperature before you call the question.

Voting

binding

The formal act: one person, one voice, published tally. Supports yes/no/abstain, ranked choice and threshold rules — with the full result trail left in the open.

06

Who it's for

Same four modules, five kinds of rooms.

Movements grassroots campaigns · advocacy networks

Turn a crowd in the street into a crowd that decides. The forum carries the debate, the assemblies shape the demands, the vote ratifies them.

modules: forum · assemblies · voting
Parliaments national · regional · legislative bodies

Public consultation built into the legislative pipeline: citizens submit and discuss proposals, committees read the temperature before readings, and votes stay on the record. Consultation that isn't theater.

modules: polling · assemblies · voting
Civic society organizations ngos · neighborhood associations · unions

Membership organizations that outgrew the annual general meeting. Continuous deliberation between assemblies, a sensed base, every member in the loop — not just those free on a weeknight.

modules: forum · polling · assemblies
Political parties party bases · internal democracy

A party's base is its oldest deliberative assembly — and usually its least wired one. Members draft the program, vote on candidates and mandates with ranked ballots, and the mood between congresses is measured, not guessed.

modules: forum · voting · polling
Municipalities cities · towns · local councils

The natural home of participatory budgeting and local consultations. Citizens propose, deliberate, and allocate real budget lines — then watch the outcome published block by block. Where the field was born; where it works best.
